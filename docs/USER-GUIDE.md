# Music Portal user guide

Music Portal (formerly Midivory) teaches piano, guitar and violin with short
lessons, and can listen to you play and tell you how it went. This guide covers
getting started, each instrument, the tuner and Piano check.

Anything marked **coming later** is planned but is not in the first Music
Portal release.

## Getting started

1. Open Music Portal on the web, on Android or on Windows, and sign in. If you
   used Midivory, sign in with the same account. Your progress and settings are
   still there.
2. The first time, you are asked **"What do you play?"** Choose piano, guitar
   or violin. You can pick more than one, and switch between them at any time
   in Settings → Instrument.
3. The instrument you are using now decides what you see in **Learn**,
   **Today** and the practice area. Your streak, experience points and
   achievements are shared across instruments, because they are all yours.
4. Open a lesson. Each one has a short explanation, music to read, a few
   exercises and sometimes a quiz. Press **Listen** on an exercise to hear it
   first.

The tuner works without an account and without an internet connection.

A note on the word "Sound": in Settings, the **Sound** section is the voice you
hear when the app plays notes back. It is separate from the instrument you
play.

## Setting up your microphone

Guitar and violin lessons, microphone piano lessons, the tuner and Piano check
all use your microphone. Music Portal analyses the sound **on your device**. It
never records it and never uploads it.

The first time, Music Portal explains what it is about to do and then your
device asks for permission. If you say no, MIDI keyboards and the on-screen
instruments still work. You can allow it later in your device settings.

A short set-up walks you through four things. You can run it again from
Settings whenever you like:

1. **Choose your microphone**, if your device has more than one.
2. **Check the level.** Play a few notes and keep the meter in the green zone.
3. **Measure the room.** Stay quiet for two seconds so the app learns your
   background noise.
4. **Measure delay.** The app plays a short click and listens for it, so it can
   judge your timing fairly. If you wear headphones, it asks you to play a note
   on each click instead.

Tips for the best results:

- Use a quiet room, and place the device about an arm's length from your
  instrument.
- Headphones help. The metronome click can leak into the microphone from the
  speakers.
- The status label tells you what is happening: "Listening", "Too quiet" or
  "Too noisy". A poor grade should never be a mystery.

## Piano

You can play piano in three ways:

- **MIDI keyboard.** Connect a USB keyboard (on Android, also Bluetooth). Notes
  are graded exactly as before, including both hands, timing and chords. On
  Windows, USB keyboards work at launch; Bluetooth MIDI on Windows is **coming
  later**.
- **On-screen piano or computer keys.** Good for trying things out.
- **Microphone, on an acoustic piano.** Play single-line melodies and the app
  grades the notes and timing. Two-hand playing and full chords are graded only
  with a MIDI keyboard.

The existing lessons, play-along and sheet features are unchanged.

## Guitar

**Reading the music.** Guitar lessons show the notes on a staff with tab
underneath. You can switch to tab only. Chords have little diagrams showing
where your fingers go. An on-screen **fretboard** lights the notes you should
play. It is for looking at and practising along, not for grading.

**How it is graded.** Play your own guitar into the microphone.

- **Single notes** are checked one by one: the right note, at the right time.
  Plucked notes fade away quickly, so Music Portal is relaxed about how long
  you hold each one.
- **Chords** are marked right or wrong as a whole. If a chord is wrong, you get
  a hint such as "sounded like Em. Check your third finger."
- The app cannot tell *which string* a note was played on, so it does not grade
  that.

**What is in the first release.** Lessons run from first steps (holding the
guitar, string names, tuning, reading tab) through open strings, first notes,
first chords and chord changes, and bass notes with strumming. Later lessons
on melodies in first position, fingerstyle, barre chords, pentatonic and blues
are **coming later**. The music is traditional or public-domain.

**Tips.** Tune first using the tuner. Strum clearly and let the chord ring for
a moment. Mute the strings you are not playing where the diagram shows a cross.

## Violin

**Reading the music.** Violin lessons show finger numbers (0 to 4), which
string to use, position markers and bowing marks for down-bow and up-bow, plus
slurs. An on-screen **fingerboard** shows where your fingers go for each new
pattern. It is display only.

**How it is graded.** Play your violin into the microphone. Besides the right
note and timing, the app tells you how many **cents** each note was sharp or
flat, so you can hear and fix your intonation. Natural vibrato is not penalised:
the app uses the centre of the note. The tolerance gets tighter as you move up
through the levels.

Playing two strings at once (double stops) is not graded in the first release.

**What is in the first release.** First steps (holding the violin and bow,
open strings, tuning), open-string bowing, the first finger pattern and simple
tunes, the second finger pattern, and rhythm and bowing. Later lessons on
two-octave scales, dynamics, third position, vibrato and double stops are
**coming later**.

**Tips.** Tune with the tuner first, using the fine tuners where you can. Play
with a steady bow so the microphone hears a clear note.

## The tuner

Open it from the instrument picker, from any microphone exercise, or straight
from the menu. It needs no account and no internet.

- Play a note. You see its name, its frequency and a needle. The green zone is
  in tune. Needle left means flat, right means sharp.
- **Hold** freezes the last reading. A small trace shows whether the pitch is
  drifting.
- Choose a **preset**. Guitar has standard tuning, Drop D, half step down, full
  step down, Drop C, DADGAD, Open G, Open D and Open E. Violin has standard
  tuning (G D A E). Piano mode covers all 88 keys. Tap a string to lock the
  tuner onto it, or let it find the nearest string.
- **Capo** (guitar): set the fret and the targets shift.
- **Reference pitch:** A4 is 440 Hz by default and can be set anywhere from 415
  to 466 Hz, with quick picks for 440, 442, 443, 432 and 415. Lessons, the tuner
  and Piano check all use your setting.
- **Reference tone:** play the target note so you can tune by ear.
- **Custom tunings** can be named and saved to your account.

## Piano check

Piano check measures an acoustic piano and shows which notes are out of tune. It
is there to help you decide whether to call a piano technician. It **does not
teach you to tune**. A guided tuning aid is **coming later**, with safety
guidance.

1. Choose **Quick check** (about 20 notes, around three minutes) or **Full
   check** (every key).
2. The app shows a key on a keyboard picture. Play it at a medium volume and let
   it ring. The app listens for about a second and a half, then moves on. You
   can redo any note.
3. Read the results:
   - A keyboard coloured **green** (within 5 cents), **amber** (5 to 15 cents)
     or **red** (more than 15 cents).
   - The **average pitch level** of the whole piano, for example "18 cents
     flat". On pianos that haven't been tuned for a while this is often the
     most useful number.
   - A list of the worst notes.
   - A plain verdict: in good shape, a tuning would help, or a pitch raise is
     likely needed.
4. Notes are judged against a typical **stretch** as well as against equal
   temperament, because a well-tuned piano is slightly stretched at both ends.
   Judging only against equal temperament would wrongly flag good notes.

The Full check report, saved history and before and after comparisons are part
of the optional upgrade. The quick check and its verdict are free.

**A word of caution.** Turning tuning pins changes the tension on strings that
carry a very large load. For most pianos the best choice is a qualified piano
technician.

Tips: close the lid or keep the room quiet, play the same way for every note,
and don't let other noise (a fan, a television) run during the check.

## Your progress and account

- **Free and optional upgrade.** The tuner, the quick Piano check and the first
  lessons for each instrument are free. Later lessons and some extra tools are
  part of an optional upgrade. If you already used Midivory, everything you
  could use before is still yours.
- **One account** works on the web, on Android and on Windows. Lessons
  finished on one device show as finished on the others.
- **Offline.** The tuner always works offline. Downloading lessons for offline
  use is **coming later**.
- **Delete your account** any time from Settings → Account, or from the delete
  account page without signing in.
- **Privacy.** Microphone sound is analysed on your device and is never
  recorded or uploaded. See the privacy page for everything we store.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Try this |
|---|---|
| "Too quiet" | Move closer, play a little louder, or raise the input level in your device's sound settings |
| "Too noisy" | Find a quieter room, switch off fans, and use headphones so the click doesn't leak into the microphone |
| The app can't hear me at all | Check the microphone permission in your device settings. On Windows: Settings → Privacy & security → Microphone |
| Notes are marked early or late | Run the microphone set-up again so the delay is measured, and try headphones |
| A note was marked wrong but you think it was right | Use the feedback button to report a wrong grade. It sends the notes the app heard, never sound |
| The tuner jumps between notes | Play one string at a time, let it ring, and mute the others |
| My MIDI keyboard isn't listed | Plug it in before starting the app, make sure no other music program is holding it open, and press re-scan |
| An old version can't open a new lesson | Update the app. It will show "Update the app to see this" |
